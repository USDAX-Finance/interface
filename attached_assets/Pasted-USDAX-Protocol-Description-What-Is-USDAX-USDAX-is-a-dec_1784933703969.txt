USDAX — Protocol Description
What Is USDAX?
USDAX is a decentralized stablecoin pegged to the US Dollar. Unlike USDT or USDC — which are backed by a company holding real dollars in a bank — USDAX is backed entirely by crypto collateral locked in a smart contract. No company controls it. No bank holds the reserves. The math does.

Architecture
USDAX runs on Robinhood Chain, a Layer 2 rollup that settles to Ethereum. It is built around three smart contracts that work together:

VaultEngine is the core. It accepts collateral (WETH, WBTC, stETH), calculates how much USDAX you can safely mint against it, and enforces all the rules — minimum debt, collateral ratios, and liquidation thresholds. Nothing moves without VaultEngine approving it.

CollateralManager handles the supported asset list and their parameters: which tokens are accepted, what the loan-to-value ratio is, and at what point a vault becomes unsafe.

MockPriceOracle feeds live asset prices into the system. The protocol continuously monitors each vault's health using these prices. If a vault drops below the safe threshold, it becomes eligible for liquidation.

USDAxSavings lets holders deposit their USDAX and earn a fixed 4.20% APY, funded by mint fees collected from vault activity.

How It Works
The mechanism is straightforward and follows the same model as MakerDAO (DAI):

You lock collateral. Deposit WETH, WBTC, or stETH into your vault. The collateral is held by the smart contract — not by any person or company.

You mint USDAX. The protocol creates new USDAX tokens and sends them to your wallet. No one transfers tokens to you — the contract prints them fresh, backed by your locked collateral. You can mint up to 75–80% of your collateral's value, depending on the asset.

A 0.5% mint fee is deducted automatically. Of the USDAX created, 0.5% goes to the protocol fee wallet — this is what funds the savings reward pool over time.

Your vault stays healthy as long as your collateral value stays above the liquidation threshold. The protocol monitors this continuously using oracle prices.

To get your collateral back, you repay the full USDAX debt. The protocol burns those tokens — permanently removing them from supply — and releases your collateral back to your wallet.

What Keeps USDAX at $1?
The peg is maintained by economic incentives, not by a central authority:

If USDAX trades below $1, vault owners are incentivized to buy cheap USDAX on the market and repay their debt, shrinking supply and pushing the price back up.
If USDAX trades above $1, new users are incentivized to open vaults and mint USDAX to sell, increasing supply and pulling the price back down.
Liquidations enforce the floor: any vault that goes undercollateralized is liquidated by external actors who receive a 5% bonus on the collateral they buy, making the protocol whole again.
Key Advantages
Non-custodial. Your collateral is locked in a smart contract, not held by a company. No one can freeze, seize, or move it — including the team that deployed the protocol.

No credit check. You do not need a bank account, credit score, or identity verification. Anyone with a wallet and crypto can open a vault.

Capital-efficient. Your locked collateral does not sit idle. You unlock liquidity from assets you already hold without selling them — useful when you want to use USDAX for spending or DeFi while maintaining long exposure to your collateral.

Transparent. Every vault, every mint, every liquidation is visible on-chain. The total USDAX supply, every holder, and every transaction is publicly verifiable — as seen on the token holders page, where all 27,610 USDAX in existence are accounted for across exactly 4 addresses.

Yield-bearing. USDAX holders can deposit into USDAxSavings and earn 4.20% APY, making idle stablecoins productive without leaving the protocol.
